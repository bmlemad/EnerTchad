In an oil project, most of a well’s cost goes to services — and in landlocked Chad, every service mobilised from abroad pays twice: the service, then the logistics and the waiting. A task force based at the worksite attacks both terms of the equation.

50-70 %

of a well’s cost typically goes to oilfield services and equipment — that is where the competitiveness of a Chadian barrel is won or lost.

4-12 wks

of international mobilisation avoided when the crew, tooling and parts are already in N’Djamena or on the basin — waiting for a tool is often more expensive than the tool.

NPT ↓

non-productive time (NPT) shrinks when diagnosis, intervention and spare parts are a few hours of track away — not a charter flight and a border away.

1 contract

integrated OFS removes cascading margins and interface costs between subcontractors: one contact, one responsibility, one price.

Our signature

The cost-reduction specialists — because we develop local content.

An intervention base at the worksite, Chadian personnel trained by the academy, inputs sourced in-country — from Kanem natron to cotton cellulose. Every localised link removes freight, foreign currency and weeks of waiting: the same service, delivered at the field, structurally costs less — and the value stays in Chad.

For an E&P operator, it is also local content documented contract by contract under the Chadian Petroleum Code: our procurement policy gives bonus scoring to bids from Chadian-registered companies, sizes lots to stay within reach of local SMEs, and builds HSE and quality capability with Tchaditude — the traceability your compliance teams expect.

Field signature · Zero spill, zero barrel lost

Specialised “frac tank”-type containers from the moment the well starts up.

From a well’s tests and clean-up flow — even before the permanent facilities — the crude is captured in specialised, adapted containers inspired by frac tanks: high-capacity mobile tanks, set on dedicated containment, connected in series on the wellpad and movable by truck.

A double function: spill protection around operations (containment, confined transfers, nothing on the ground) and immediate commercial recovery — every test barrel is stored, measured and evacuated instead of burnt or lost. The well pays from the day it starts up.

The capture fleet · Removable containers on wheels

The right tank, at the right well, at the right time.

Six families of mobile containers cover every moment of the well’s life, from the first test barrel to long-distance evacuation — all sit on containment, connect in series and leave by road.

00 · Separation at the head

Mobile three-phase separator

The prerequisite of all capture: a containerised skid separates gas, crude and water at the wellhead — a heater-treater (heater-treater) for heavy crudes, metering at the outlet. The crude leaves on spec (BS&W < 0.5%) to the tanks, the water to treatment, the gas is measured instead of flared.

20-foot skid · 2,000–20,000 b/d — set by crane truck
01 · Main storage

Towable 500 bbl frac tank

The wellpad’s workhorse: a corrugated-wall steel tank on skids, integrated rear axle. Connected in series on containment, opening roof, optional heating coils for paraffinic crudes. Moved empty from well to well.

500 bbl · ≈ 79 m³ — towed empty (winch truck)
02 · Storage + road evacuation

Road-approved frac tank trailer

Same capacity, mounted as a multi-axle semi-trailer: it captures test crude for weeks on containment, then leaves directly by road for the mini-refinery. Axle load to validate against track conditions.

500 bbl · ≈ 79 m³ — road semi-trailer
03 · Active recovery

130 bbl vacuum truck

An onboard vacuum pump: it draws directly from the well cellar, containment bottoms and test tanks, with no external pump. The “zero barrel lost” tool — drips, clean-up flow, loaded fluids. Fast rotation.

130 bbl · ≈ 21 m³ — onboard vacuum pump
04 · Multi-well rotation

Hook-lift skip

A sealed hook tank, set and retrieved in minutes by a standard hook-lift truck: one carrier serves several wells, full skips settle on site. Cut out for marginal wells in re-entry.

20–110 bbl — set/retrieved in minutes
05 · Multimodal logistics

26 m³ ISO tank container

A stainless tank in a 20-foot container frame: twist-locks, double valve, flame arrestors. Moves from road chassis to flatbed truck, even to rail — the long-distance evacuation option to the refinery or export.

≈ 165 bbl · 26 m³ — road, flatbed, rail
06 · First test barrels

Towable double-wall tank

A small tank with built-in containment on a light trailer, towable by pick-up and movable full at low speed on the pad. The buffer for the very first barrels of a well test, at the test separator outlet.

12–50 bbl · 2–8 m³ — towable by pick-up
Typical fleet · Wellpad at start-up

A mobile three-phase separator at the head (the right crude on spec, water and gas diverted), two 500 bbl frac tanks connected in series on containment (main storage), a 130 bbl vacuum truck in rotation (active recovery and evacuation), two to three hook-lift skips for satellite wells, a double-wall tank for the initial test phase.

Common specifications: heating coils (paraffinic crudes), vents with flame arrestors, reinforced ground clearance and sand tyres, foldable soft containments that travel with the equipment.

Contracts & standards · Working with us

Four contract models, one non-negotiable HSE-Q foundation.

Per job. One intervention, one work order, one price — ideal for one-off needs and well emergencies.
Framework contract (call-off). Rates and SLAs agreed for the year, mobilisation on demand — predictability for campaigns.
Success-based (EOR). Payment indexed to the additional barrels recovered — we share the technical risk with the operator.
Alliance / joint venture. Long-term integration on a scope: a dedicated team, shared learning, measured local content.
The HSE-Q foundation

Systematic work permits, LOTO lockout, stop-work authority for every worker, pre-task risk analyses, a bridging document aligned with the operator’s standard. Well barriers and kick control: BOPs tested on schedule, well-control drills, IWCF/IADC certification targeted for all personnel on pressurised operations. ISO 9001 · 14001 · 45001 approach targeted, HSE reporting shared with the client. (targeted framework · company in formation)
