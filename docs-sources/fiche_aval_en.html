The modular, movable mini-refinery

  • Standardised trains: from 500 to 2,000 b/d, prefabricated and skid-mounted, 100% catalogue parts; capacity added module by module, at the pace of demand. Trains deployed in stages; cumulative capacity set after the feasibility study.
  • Movable and redeployable: the unit would be transported, set down and lifted again without heavy civil works, then moved towards satellite blocks and markets as demand requires.
  • Maintenance by module swap: replacement of a module targeted in under 24 h, drawn from a shared catalogue stock.
  • A deliberately simple first stage: atmospheric distillation, with neither cracking nor hydrotreating — products that require those units remain imported at this stage. Frugality applies to the architecture, not to the critical steels, which are corrosion-resistant for Doba’s acidic crude.

Launch model · B2B

The modular mini-refinery is a dated target, not a day-one capacity. At launch: import of finished products (Niger, Nigeria, Cameroon, Angola, Algeria), stored in three hubs — N’Djamena, Moundou, Abéché — and delivered to professionals: mining & construction, hauliers & fleets, agro-industry, backup power & institutions.

Target network

12 fixed Tchadium stations across 8 strategic localities, backed by 3 depot hubs; the Mobile Stations would extend coverage to landlocked areas — at the ARSAT price everywhere.

Four segments

The pump · Industry and fleets · Aviation (Jet A-1) · LPG (household cylinder and industrial gas). Targeted brands and programmes: EnerClub, EnerPro Card, NRJ+, EnerLub.

ARSAT price

Pump prices are set by ARSAT (Autorité de Régulation du Secteur pétrolier Aval, the downstream petroleum regulator). EnerTchad does not set prices: its role as an operator is to apply them everywhere, in town and in remote areas alike, adding nothing to the official scale.
