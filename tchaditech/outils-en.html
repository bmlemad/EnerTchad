Maintenance 4.0 · Operating profitability

What predictive maintenance changes for profitability.

Beyond the technology (detailed in the Technology & Future section), the stake is economic: anticipating failures rather than repairing after the breakdown cuts shutdowns, parts waste and OPEX — and directly improves the cost per barrel.

Predictive, not curative

Sensors and AI detect wear before the failure: we intervene at the right time, not in a rush.

Fewer shutdowns

Maximised asset availability: every avoided hour of downtime is production preserved.

Optimised inventory

Parts are ordered just-in-time, reducing tied-up capital and waste.

Cost per barrel under control

Reduced operating expenses (OPEX) that directly strengthen the profitability of every operation.

Day to day

What the digital stack changes, role by role

A tool stack is worth only what it changes in the day of the people who use it — customer, station manager, depot operator, reservoir engineer.

Customer

Order without queueing

Mobile ordering and payment, a collection slot, a notification when the product is ready: customers no longer discover a stock-out at the pump.

Station manager

Run the site on live data

Telemetered tank levels, sales consolidated the same evening, variance alerts: the manager sees the station as a dashboard, no longer as a black box.

Depot operator

Trace every batch

Receipt, metering and allocation captured once, in the right place: traceability stops being a stack of slips to re-key.

Reservoir engineer

Decide on the data

Data historian, digital twin, well telemetry: the decline curve is read continuously, not once a quarter.

Management

A single true figure

The same data feeds operations, accounting and reporting: no more three versions of the same volume.
