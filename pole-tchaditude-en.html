The context · why this hub

The challenges of Chadian oil human capital.

Building an integrated oil company in Chad first requires trained women and men — from a scarce base. Here are the six obstacles the Human capital hub must clear, ahead of the first barrel.

  • 01 · ChallengeA scarce next generation, to be trained almost from scratchGeosciences, drilling, reservoir, HSE, refining: the crude trades lack locally trained talent. Expertise has long been imported — energy sovereignty starts by reversing that dependence.
  • 02 · ChallengeKeeping talent in the countryQualified profiles leave for regional operators or abroad. The challenge is not only to train, but to offer career paths that make people want to build the sector here.
  • 03 · ChallengeBridging the gap between university and the fieldGeneral academic training prepares poorly for the practical skills of oil work. Without a bridge to practice — internships, technical platforms, work-study — the degree stays far from the site.
  • 04 · ChallengePassing on know-how that is barely documentedThe experienced generation is thin, and its tacit knowledge is lost from one site to the next. Without organised apprenticeship or mentoring, every project relearns what the previous one already knew.
  • 05 · ChallengeEquipping local content, beyond the obligationEmploying and training nationals is not only a regulatory requirement: it is an HR undertaking to be structured stream by stream, with skills frameworks and certifying pathways.
  • 06 · ChallengePreparing digital professions that are rarer stillThe digital-native operator needs cross-cutting skills — data, AI, sensors and the field. That hybrid profile is almost impossible to find locally: it has to be built, not waited for.

Company in formation: these challenges frame the hub’s ambition, they do not describe assets in operation.
