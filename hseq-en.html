HSE-Q · Health · Safety · Environment · Quality

Safety comes before production.

Before the first barrel, EnerTchad builds its HSE-Q culture: a stop-work authority granted to everyone, an ALARP logic (risk as low as reasonably practicable) and targeted alignment with the major international frameworks. (target bedrock · certification targets, company in formation)

H
Health

Occupational health, industrial hygiene and decent site conditions — subcontractors included.

S
Safety

Stop-work authority, work permits, risk management and robotics to take people out of dangerous zones.

E
Environment

End of routine flaring targeted, produced-water management, site and corridor integrity.

Q
Quality

Products up to standard (fuels, LPG, bitumen), traceability and continuous improvement.

Water management · A critical resource

Water, the critical resource at the heart of the Sahel.

Chad lives under chronic water stress. EnerTchad makes water a cross-cutting ESG priority: abstract as little fresh water as possible, reuse in a loop, and never return anything to the environment without treatment and control. Four strands — sourcing, produced water, wastewater, communities — under continuous surveillance. (target system · company in formation)

Water footprint & sourcing
Minimal abstractionPrefer brackish/non-potable water; never compete with people’s water.
Rainwater harvestingCollection on sites and roofs for non-noble uses (washing, dust control).
Metering & footprintMeasuring every m³ abstracted and reused — water intensity per barrel tracked.
No over-exploited aquiferNo capture of communities’ vital resources; prior impact studies.

HSE-Q · Operational biodiversity

Protecting wildlife and flora, site by site.

In Chad, oil activity sits alongside fragile environments — Sahelian savannahs, the wetlands of the Logone and the Chari, the deserts of the north. EnerTchad writes the protection of the living world into the very design of its sites, following the hierarchy avoid → reduce → restore.

01 · Avoid

Siting that goes around the living world

Impact study (ESIA) and fauna-flora inventory before any worksite; mapping of sensitive habitats; siting outside protected areas (Zakouma, OROA, Ennedi) and outside migration corridors.

02 · Reduce

The smallest possible footprint

Modular mini-refineries and mobile assets = less land sealed. Flaring and light pollution kept in check (migratory birds), tracks controlled against poaching, strict waste and hydrocarbon management, zero abstraction from critical aquifers.

03 · Restore

Returning the site to its environment

Revegetation with local species (Acacia gum trees, Sahelian flora), soil restoration and long-term biodiversity monitoring — with a “no net loss” target.

Species & environments under watch — Chad’s natural heritage
Scimitar-horned oryx & addaxReintroduced into the Ouadi Rimé–Ouadi Achim reserve (78,000 km²); the oryx, once “extinct in the wild”, again counts ~700 individuals — the largest population in the world.
Elephants & Kordofan giraffeZakouma National Park (managed by African Parks) — a heritage that any neighbouring activity must preserve.
Birdlife & wetlandsLake Chad and the Lakes of Ounianga (UNESCO): major migratory stopovers, sensitive to noise, light and water.
Sahelian floraGum trees, acacias and pasture: refining close to the field reduces the land footprint and preserves plant cover.
Open governanceEnerTchad is targeting partnerships with protected-area managers and conservation NGOs, published biodiversity indicators, and fauna-flora monitoring folded into HSE-Q reporting.

HSE-Q · Air quality

Air under control, from well to pump.

Oil touches the air through four routes: flaring, methane leaks, worksite dust and combustion gases. EnerTchad aims to measure them and cut them at the source, not offset them afterwards.

Zéroroutine flaring — target
LDARleak detection & repair

Continuousambient-air monitoring (target)
01 · Flaring

Burn the least, use the rest

“Zero routine flaring” target: associated gas is captured for site power (gas-solar hybrid), the mini-refineries and the oil towns, rather than burnt at the flare.

02 · Methane & leaks

Hunting the invisible (LDAR)

A leak detection and repair programme (LDAR) on valves, flanges and compressors; optical gas cameras and sensors — methane is a greenhouse gas 80× more potent than CO₂ in the short term.

03 · Dust

Keeping the ground footprint in check

Track watering and stabilisation, limited speeds, covered transport: the reduced footprint of the modular mini-refineries already limits the dust raised in the Sahelian zone.

04 · Ambient air

Measuring to prove

Monitoring of pollutants (SO₂, NOₓ, particulates) around sensitive sites and neighbouring villages; data folded into HSE-Q reporting and published.

Doba’s acidic crude demands rigourIts sulphur content (high TAN) dictates the metallurgy and gas treatment: air quality is decided at unit design, sulphur recovered rather than released.

HSE-Q · Process safety

Preventing the accident, not recording it.

Occupational safety protects the person; process safety prevents the major event — leak, fire, explosion. EnerTchad designs it upstream, through independent barriers, verified and maintained over time.

Analyse

Hunting the hazard on paper

HAZOP and LOPA studies from the design stage: dreaded scenarios are identified and protection layers sized before anything is built.

Control

Nothing changes without sign-off

Management of change (MOC) and work permits: no process modification or hot work without a risk analysis and a traced authorisation.

Shut down

The reflex that saves

Safety instrumented systems (SIS) and emergency shutdowns (ESD): automatic tripping to a safe state, independent of the control system, tested periodically.

Measure

The right signals, not slogans

Tracking of process-safety events (Tier 1 / Tier 2, API 754) and of barrier health — the indicator that anticipates the major accident.

Sustainability · Operational safety

Safety, the licence to operate.

In oil, HSE is not an annex: it is the condition of the barrel. EnerTchad models its target arrangements on the industry’s international standards (IOGP), adapted to Chadian terrain — six disciplines that will apply on every site, from well to pump.

The eight Golden Rules

Work permits, working at height, confined spaces, energy lockout (LOTO), lifting, excavation, road safety, management of change: no job starts without them.

Measuring to prevent

Bird’s pyramid guides the vigilance: every near-miss reported without blame prevents tomorrow’s accident. TRIR and LTIF tracked from the first worksite, with one non-negotiable objective: zero major accidents.

Permits & barriers

No hazardous work without a signed permit. A risk analysis (JSA) before every task, and defence-in-depth barriers — technical and organisational — designed on the bowtie model.

Prepared for the worst

A site-specific emergency plan, regular drills, firefighting resources sized to the products handled, and a medical-evacuation chain organised despite the landlocked Chadian theatre.

Health, ranked the same

Industrial hygiene (H₂S, benzene, noise, Sahelian heat), medical surveillance of the teams, malaria prevention extended to neighbouring communities: health is managed like safety.

A culture, not a binder

HSE induction before any site access, a daily safety minute, and stop-work authority recognised for everyone, from intern to director: the rule protects whoever invokes it.
