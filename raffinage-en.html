Downstream · Our trump card

The modular & removable mini-refinery

Our trump card for reaching our objectives: a refinery that is modern, modular, removable and small-scale, designed in Chad for our realities. Standardised 500 to 2,000 b/d trains, prefabricated and skid-mounted, 100 % catalogue parts. Where a mega-refinery is rigid and out of reach, our unit deploys, redeploys and upgrades crude as close as possible to the satellite blocks — a mobile asset, never stranded. (ambition · company in formation)

Modular

Standardised trains assembled in the factory, then hooked up on site like building blocks.

  • Prefabricated trains of 500 to 2,000 b/d.
  • Capacity added module by module, at the pace of demand.
  • 100 % catalogue parts — no costly custom builds.
  • Exception: anti-corrosion steels for Doba's acidic crude — frugality applies to the architecture, not to critical materials.
Use case · cost reduction

Challenge — A mega-refinery is rigid, oversized and financially out of reach.

Integrated answer — Standardised 500–2,000 b/d trains, 100 % catalogue parts, capacity added module by module.

Removable

Skid-mounted: the unit is transported, set down and lifted again without heavy civil works.

  • Skid chassis transportable by road.
  • Minimal ground footprint, light foundations.
  • Dismantling and lifting without a permanent worksite.

The concept at a glance

Anatomy of the model: a short chain, on a skid

A modular mini-refinery is not a large refinery in miniature: it is a short chain, sized for local demand, designed to be assembled, operated — and moved. Here, at a single glance, is how Doba crude becomes fuels, gas and bitumen.

↔ Scroll the diagram horizontally.

Frugal capex, staged in tiers

Where a mega-refinery demands colossal capital in one go, the modular model invests train by train: you pay for capacity at the pace demand justifies. No oversizing, no asset frozen in concrete.

Atmospheric distillation, simple and robust

A proven process, few units, 100% catalogue parts: maintenance is done by swapping a module (target < 24 h) rather than a lengthy shutdown. Frugality is in the architecture — not in the critical steels, anti-corrosion for Doba’s acidic crude.

Anchored to the upstream

Crude arrives from nearby satellite blocks: a short chain, less transport, value captured close to the well. When a field declines, the unit lifts off and redeploys to the next.

Less crude exported, less fuel imported

Every barrel refined on site is a litre that does not leave for export only to return imported at a premium. The model tackles Chad’s paradox head-on: producing oil yet enduring expensive fuels.

How it works

From crude to product: the mini-refinery, step by step

A modular mini-refinery is not a large refinery in miniature: it is a short chain, sized for local demand, designed to be assembled, operated — and moved. Here is its journey.

Step 1

Receiving & preparation

Crude arrives from the nearby field, is metered, settled and stripped of its water and sediments: clean crude protects the whole chain downstream.

Step 2

Heating & distillation

Brought up to temperature, the crude rises in the atmospheric column: each cut — gas, gasolines, kerosene, diesel, residue — separates by boiling point.

Step 3

Cuts & specifications

Each cut is stabilised, blended and checked in the laboratory against national specifications: an off-spec product never leaves the site.

Step 4

Valorising the residue

The column bottoms are not waste: heavy fuel oil for industry and power plants, bitumen base for roads — every fraction finds its use.

Step 5

Storage & dispatch

Dedicated tanks per product, custody metering at loading, batch traceability: the mini-refinery supplies the Downstream depots, with the numbers to prove it.

Step 6

Redeploying the module

When local demand shifts or a field declines, the unit is dismantled and rebuilt elsewhere: capital follows the need — that is the whole point of “movable”.

Downstream · The mini-refinery range

Three models, one DNA: refine as close to the need as possible.

The Mobile Station™ philosophy applied to refining: standardised 500–2,000 b/d trains, assembled into three configurations depending on the territory to serve — and expandable train by train, without shutting the unit down. (target range · company in formation)

MR-500 · “Pioneer”

The unit that opens a basin

1 train · 500 b/d · containerisable

  • Diesel and kerosene for the immediate market of an isolated block or early production.
  • From factory to first litre in 6–9 months; redeployable in weeks.
  • Small crew, remote supervision from the TchadiTech centre.
Marginal blocksEarly production
MR-1000 · “Basin”

The regional backbone

2 trains · 1,000–2,000 b/d

  • EN 590 diesel, RON 95 gasoline, kerosene/Jet A-1 — the everyday range.
  • Coupled to a hub depot: clean storage, cascading distribution to the stations.
  • Capacity added train by train, with no operational shutdown.
Producing basinsHub depots
MR-2000 · “Hub+”

The compact complex

3+ trains · up to 6,000 b/d

  • Bitumen units (60/70 · 80/100) and LPG alongside the fuels.
  • Designed for Doba’s heavy, acidic crude (TAN 4.7): adapted metallurgy.
  • Prepares the petrochemical stage and feeds the station corridor.

Ultra-modern by design

A refinery born digital, not retrofitted.

No legacy systems to drag along: every unit ships from day one with the technology layer that large complexes bolt on afterwards.

01 · Digital twin

Every train has its double

A living digital replica of the unit: test a cut, a feed, a maintenance operation in the virtual world before touching the real one. The TchadiTech backbone →

02 · Yield AI

Cuts follow demand

Machine-learning optimisation adjusts diesel/gasoline/kerosene yields to local demand and crude profile — each barrel earns more, with no new capex.

03 · Remote operations

SCADA, sensors, robots

Continuous supervision, predictive maintenance and inspection robotics (tanks, flares, lines): more monitoring, less human exposure.

04 · Clean process energy

Zero routine flaring

Process gas powers the unit (heat, electricity), hybridised with on-site solar — a refinery that produces energy without wasting it. GreenTech →

05 · Closed water loop

Water-to-Value™ applied

Process water treated and reused, discharges controlled: in a water-stressed Chad, the refinery gives back the water it borrows.

06 · Instrumented safety

Safe by design

HAZOP/LOPA studies from the design stage, safety-instrumented systems (SIS) and tested emergency shutdowns (ESD) — big-complex standards at skid scale.

The key to the vision

“Access to Energies” starts at the refinery.

Refining locally, as close to the need as possible, is what makes everything else work: the regulated ARSAT price held everywhere, shortages cushioned by the depots, and the everyday products — diesel, gasoline, LPG, bitumen, Jet A-1 — made from Chadian crude rather than imported from 1,700 km away. The mini-refinery feeds the hub stations and Mobile Stations™, supplies the product range, secures the distribution cascade — and prepares the petrochemical stage. It is the link that turns a slogan into a network. Discuss the model →

Quality · Proof to the litre

Specifications, certificates — and the assumed limits of the first tier.

Specifications & CoA

Every cut targets named specifications — harmonised African references (AFRI/ARSO) and EN 228 / EN 590 equivalents in sight, target sulphur content per product. Every shipped batch leaves with its laboratory certificate of analysis, retained witness samples — the same chain of proof as the corridor's custody.

Unit HSE

Managed flare and systematic hot-work permits, containment under every piece of equipment, and effluents looped into the group's water management — the mini-refinery applies to finished products the Water-to-Value™ discipline born in the field.

What the first tier does not do

No cracking, no hydrotreating: the first tier is an atmospheric distillation, deliberately simple. Products that require those units remain imported at this stage — stated plainly, as for multimodal. Further tiers will come when volumes justify them. (ambition · company in formation)
