For the investor · Why Chad, why now

A proven rent, half unexplored — and already linked to the sea.

Chad has produced since 2003 and exports through a pipeline already built. Yet 38 of the 57 blocks of its mining domain remain open. For an investor that is rare: the country-infrastructure risk is lifted, the geological potential is not.

01

A proven resource, not a speculative one

Doba produces; Sédigui (~150 Mb) waits; Doséo, Salamat and Erdis remain to be explored. Chadian oil exists — the task is to extract more of it.

02

The export route is already there

The Doba–Kribi pipeline (1,070 km) and a floating terminal: the road to the world market is built and amortised. No evacuation mega-CAPEX to carry.

03

Two blocks in three are open

38 open blocks out of 57 in the 2025 registry. A rare entry ground for anyone wanting to build a position from the start.

04

A readable frame

Concession or production sharing (PSC), the State’s share indexed on profitability (R-Factor), aligned with CEMAC standards.

1

Geological frame

Chad’s subsurface belongs to the Central African intracontinental rift system, formed in the Early Cretaceous along the Central African Shear Zone (CASZ). Extension created a series of half-grabens — Doba, Bongor, Doséo, Salamat and Termit — filled with lacustrine and fluvial sediments rich in source rocks.

The reservoirs are mainly Cretaceous sandstones (syn- and post-rift) between 1,000 and 2,500 m deep; locally, fractured granitic basement forms a secondary reservoir (Bongor basin). Crudes range from heavy and discounted (21–24° API at Doba) to lighter (~31° API at Bongor).

Doba basinPRODUCTION

The historical production heartland (since 2003) over ~200,000 km², separated from the Bongor basin by a basement high within the Central African rift. Cretaceous sandstone reservoirs at 1,200-1,800 m. Komé, Miandoum, Bolobo, Badila and Mangara fields. Heavy 21-24° API crudes, discounted against Brent — hence the stake of enhanced recovery. 3

Chad’s producing fields

Production is historically concentrated in the Doba basin (Komé, Miandoum and Bolobo fields, operated since 2003) and in the Bongor basin, whose crude feeds the Djermaya refinery through the Ronier–Djermaya pipeline. The Badila and Mangara fields round out the South’s productive portfolio.

6

Infrastructure & export

Export rests on the Doba–Kribi pipeline, about 1,070 km long (of which ~170 km on Chadian territory), commissioned in 2003 — operated by TOTCO on the Chadian side and COTCO on the Cameroonian side. SHT holds 40% of TOTCO; fiscal metering (LACT, Coriolis meters ±0.05%) certifies transfers at Doba, Komé and Kribi. The crude is loaded off Kribi (Cameroon) via the Komé–Kribi 1 floating terminal. Refining & Distribution, the Djermaya refinery (SRN, 20 kb/d nominal, ~14 kb/d actual), commissioned in 2011 with CNPC, remains the country’s only refining site.

Gas & safety — H₂S and associated gas

Doba crude is sweet, but H₂S remains an operating risk — associated gas, confined spaces, and reservoir souring induced by water injection / EOR. Heavier than air, it accumulates in low points.

Legal & tax regime

The sector is framed by the Hydrocarbons Code, under which concession and production-sharing contract (PSC) coexist. The regime sets royalties, oil taxation and the State’s share; its stability conditions long-term investment.

LEGAL REGIME
Concession & PSC

Chad runs two regimes side by side: the concession and the production-sharing contract (PSC), framed by the Hydrocarbons Code.

PRODUCTION SHARING
The R-Factor mechanism

The State’s share evolves with the field’s cumulative profitability (R-Factor), a readable frame aligned with CEMAC standards.

TAXATION

History

2003
Commercial production begins

First barrel exported to Kribi through the Doba–Kribi pipeline. Initial production 140 kb/d (Bolobo, Miandoum, Komé). Peak of 210 kb/d in 2004.

2011
The Djermaya refinery comes on stream (SRN)

The first national refining capacity, 20 kb/d. Chad enters the oil downstream.

2014
EITI compliance & sector structuring

Chad becomes compliant with the 2011 EITI standard — transparency of oil flows to the State.

2023
Asset nationalisation & law 009/PT/2023

Nationalisation of the ex-Esso assets, creation of the Tchad Petroleum Company (TPC). The State takes back the value chain.

2025
The “Tchad Connexion 2030” plan

Goal: lifting production to 250,000 barrels/day (vs ~144 today), through Doba EOR and the Salamat and Termit frontier basins. 30 bn USD of investment targeted.

2026
EnerTchad — a private Chadian integrated operator

Formation of an integrated oil operator with Chadian capital. From source rock to the pump.
