Infrastructure & export

Export rests on the Doba–Kribi pipeline, about 1,070 km long (of which ~170 km on Chadian territory), commissioned in 2003 — operated by TOTCO on the Chadian side and COTCO on the Cameroonian side. SHT holds 40% of TOTCO; fiscal metering (LACT, Coriolis meters ±0.05%) certifies transfers at Doba, Komé and Kribi. The crude is loaded off Kribi (Cameroon) via the Komé–Kribi 1 floating terminal. Refining & Distribution, the Djermaya refinery (SRN, 20 kb/d nominal, ~14 kb/d actual), commissioned in 2011 with CNPC, remains the country’s only refining site.

Crude technical sheet (Doba Blend)

The input datum of any downstream operation: the crude’s profile. Doba Blend is heavy, sweet, but marked by strong naphthenic acidity — which dictates unit metallurgy more than sulphur does.

Indicative Doba Blend profile — public assays
Source fieldsKomé · Miandoum · Bolobo (Doba basin)
Density≈ 21° API — a heavy crude
Sulphur≈ 0.10% m — doux (sweet)
Acid number (TAN)high — naphthenic acids (narrows the refiner base)
Calcium250 – 350 ppm
Stake no. 1Naphthenic corrosion → alloyed metallurgy, no major H₂S problem

Indicative values varying by batch/blend date. Sources: Chevron/Texaco assays (Oil & Gas Journal), ESMAP.

Gas & safety — H₂S and associated gas

Doba crude is sweet, but H₂S remains an operating risk — associated gas, confined spaces, and reservoir souring induced by water injection / EOR. Heavier than air, it accumulates in low points.

H₂S — concentration & effect (NIOSH/OSHA references)
ConcentrationEffect / benchmark
0.01 – 0.1 ppmOdour threshold (rotten egg) — not to be relied on
10 ppmRecommended exposure limit (NIOSH REL)

100 ppmIDLH — immediate danger; olfactory paralysis
500 ppmRapid loss of consciousness
≥ 1000 ppmImmediate collapse, one to two breaths
PropertiesDensity 1.19 (heavier than air) · flammable (LEL 4.3% – UEL 46%).
BarriersFixed + portable detection, SCBA/PPE, evacuation plans, H₂S training, souring surveillance.

Reference frame: NIOSH Pocket Guide, OSHA 1910.1000. Target of a company in formation.

In Chad, the calendar commands the operations. Drilling, crude transport and worksites are planned around the dry season; the rains and the harmattan dictate buffer stocks and maintenance.
