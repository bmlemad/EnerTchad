Our purpose · Vision & Mission

Supporting theenergy autonomy of Chad.

Beyond extraction, EnerTchad is structuring a complete, commercial processing chain for oil and gas products — from well to finished product. Our goal is not to export Chadian crude: it is to build the entire value chain in Chad — process, distribute, industrialise — with an approach that is innovative and economically profitable. Chad exports most of its crude and imports its fuels: we are reversing that logic. Serve Chadians first; export the surplus — as finished products rather than crude. To achieve this at controlled cost, we will deploy modern, compact and largely automated industrial units.

Hydrocarbons are the heart of our business and our vision: it is their upgrading in Chad that finances access to all forms of energy — from the litre of diesel to the solar kilowatt-hour.

Enhanced recovery · EOR · The barrel left in the rock · Solution

Recover 2 to 3× more from every reservoir.

Primary and secondary recovery only extract 20-35 % of the oil in place (OOIP). Input autonomy · Putting Chadian resources to work

What if EOR additives came from Chadian soil?

ASP processes rely on costly imported chemicals. Yet Chad holds natural resources directly usable as EOR inputs — cutting import dependence, creating rural value chains, and anchoring industrial autonomy.

PROVEN

Natron → alkaline agent (the A in ASP)

Na₂CO₃·NaHCO₃·2H₂O · Lake Chad & Borkou

Locally mined natron as the alkaline agent for ASP injection, replacing imported sodium carbonate. Its buffering effect lowers interfacial tension with less rock damage than NaOH.

+8-17 % OOIP tertiary recovery
PROMISING

Gum arabic → biopolymer

Acacia senegal · 42,000 t/yr · world no. 2

Polysaccharide base for xanthan-type biopolymers offering superior stability in high-salinity/high-temperature reservoirs where synthetic HPAM fails. Supports 500,000+ rural households.

HT/HS stability superior to HPAM
PROMISING

Cotton cellulose → polymer (P)

150-180,000 t/yr · 94 % cellulose

Chadian cotton (250,000 ha, 2.5 M workers) supplies pure fibres. Low-grade cotton converts into cellulosic polymers (CMC, HEC) for viscosity control, replacing petroleum-based polyacrylamide.

Modern technologies · Heavy crude

Produce more, from the existing heavy fields.

Doba’s heavy crude (21-24° API) is viscous and hard to mobilise. EnerTchad combines the latest recovery technologies — advanced chemistry, intelligent lift, AI-driven drilling and digital twins — to raise the recovery rate of mature fields, without waiting for new discoveries.

ADVANCED CHEMISTRY
Combined viscosity reduction

ASP systems reinforced with emulsifying surfactants and polymers: crude viscosity drops by more than 90 %, improving cold mobility — less energy and fewer emissions than thermal methods.

NANOFLUIDS
Polymers & nano-additives

Biopolymers and nano-additives that stay stable in hot, saline environments where conventional polymers fail — a wider, longer-lasting reservoir sweep.

INTELLIGENT LIFT
Optimised ESP-PCP pumps

Electric progressive-cavity pumps, designed for heavy crude, driven by a digital platform: better recovery, lower OPEX and longer equipment life.

AI-DRIVEN DRILLING
Horizontal & smart wells

AI-guided directional drilling and instrumented wells (downhole sensors): more precise trajectories, maximised reservoir contact and drilling speed improved by up to +30 %.

WELL STIMULATION
Acidising & fracturing

Matrix acidising and fracturing to restore and improve permeability around the well: clogged drains are reopened and mature-well productivity is revived at low cost.

STEAM STIMULATION
CSS — huff & puff

Cyclic steam injection (huff-and-puff) to heat and thin the heavy crude around the well, then produce it: a recovery gain of +20 to 50 % in Doba’s most viscous zones.

DIGITAL TWINS
AI simulation & optimisation

A digital replica of the field to test production scenarios and predict yield before going to the field — in liaison with our R&D & Technology department.

CO₂-EOR · LOW-CARBON
Miscible injection & capture

Injection of CO₂ captured at the refinery to mobilise residual crude: additional recovery paired with geological carbon storage. Under study.

Chemical autonomy · From raw material to petroleum product

Chad’s subsoil holds what the industry imports.

Wyoming bentonite, Gulf cement, North American proppants: every chemical input crosses three continents before reaching the wellhead — logistics accounts for up to 40 % of the delivered price. EnerTchad reverses the equation: extract, process and formulate locally the 9 families of raw materials found on the territory, to build Central Africa’s first integrated petroleum chemistry industry.

LEVER 01

Industrial autonomy

Produce locally what the subsoil holds in abundance. Reduce import dependence and create a petroleum chemistry pole that did not exist before.

LEVER 02

Economic competitiveness

A tonne of imported bentonite exceeds 800 USD delivered to N’Djamena; activated locally, it comes to ~350 USD. Over 20 wells, the savings run into millions of dollars.

LEVER 03

Circular economy

Produced water, flared gas and agricultural residues stop being losses and become inputs. The Water-to-Value™ turns the biggest cost item into a revenue source.

Doctrine 01 · Resilience

The distributed reserve: turning a geographic weakness into strategic strength.

Chad is a landlocked country. Its fuel travels thousands of kilometres before reaching the pump, and the slightest break in the import chain — a closed border, a road cut off in the rainy season, regional tension — can leave whole regions dry. A single central depot, however large, remains a point of failure: if it goes down, the whole country stops.

EnerTchad reverses the logic. Rather than a single vulnerable point, the midstream link aims for a mesh of regional depots: each covers its own area, each backs up the others. Together, these nodes form a distributed national reserve — physical insurance against supply shocks, sized to cover several weeks of consumption even when imports stop. Resilience stops being a cost: it becomes a strategic advantage.

Doctrine 02 · Corridor

The export corridor, artery of energy autonomy.

Chadian crude reaches the ocean by a single axis of more than a thousand kilometres, from producing basin to ocean terminal. This artery conditions the country’s entire oil economy: its reliability decides how much value Chad actually captures on each barrel produced. Irregular transit means production stuck on the ground and value lost.

The midstream link’s ambition is to maximise its use and predictability: fine-grained lifting schedules, regulated access, upstream-downstream coordination so that every barrel produced finds its route without waiting. The better the corridor is orchestrated, the more the whole chain breathes — and the more value stays in Chad instead of dissolving into logistics costs and transit penalties.

Doctrine 03 · Integrity

Integrity through data, not luck.

A pipeline, depots and isolated sites are not secured by occasional inspection: they are actively managed. EnerTchad aims for integrity management based on real-time data — pressure and flow sensors, early anomaly detection, predictive rather than corrective maintenance. A leak anticipated costs a thousand times less than a leak endured, in money as in the environment.

That same data feeds HSE safety and facility protection: knowing, at every moment, the state of every link means protecting people, the environment and the national asset. The discipline of measurement turns a regulatory constraint into a lasting operational advantage.

Doctrine 04 · Last mile

The last mile, where autonomy becomes concrete.

Moving large volumes by pipeline is efficient; reaching the last community rarely is. Yet it is there, on the last mile, that access to energy is won or lost. EnerTchad aims for multimodal logistics combining the modes — the pipeline for large flows, road and regional depots for fine-grained coverage — to also serve the areas that imports neglect.

By anchoring transport to a network of distributed depots rather than a heavy idle fleet, the model stays light in capital and rich in flexibility: the molecule moves the shortest distance, is stored as close as possible, and delivered just right. The midstream link thus becomes the invisible hyphen between the Chadian well and the citizen’s purchasing power.

Economics · Cost-to-serve

Pipeline, road, storage: three levers, one equation.

The midstream link does not choose between modes: it composes them. Thepipeline excels at large volumes at the lowest unit cost, but it is rigid and only serves its entry points. The road is more expensive per barrel, but it is what reaches the last community and absorbs the unexpected. Finally, storagedecouples production from consumption: it cushions shocks, smooths prices and makes it possible to buy at the right time.

The art of the link is to minimise the cost-to-serve end to end — not the cost of an isolated segment. Move the molecule the shortest way by pipeline, store it as close as possible in a regional depot, bring out the road only for the last mile: that orchestration is what preserves margin along the chain and, ultimately, protects the price Chadians pay at the pump. Every franc saved in logistics is a franc returned to purchasing power or reinvested in the country.

Sustainability · Wind

Wind: a real resource, where the wind blows.

Chad has more than a solar resource. The Saharan north — Borkou-Ennedi-Tibesti — carries sustained winds that GreenTech aims to couple with solar to firm up the power of remote sites, day and night.

Saharan north (BET) · strong

Sustained winds, in the range of 7 to 8 m/s across the massifs and corridors. The country’s best wind potential — priority target for off-grid hybrid mini-grids.

Sahelian belt · moderate

A seasonal regime driven by theharmattan (~3.5–4 m/s). A complement to solar on the demand centres, not an engine on its own.

Sudanian south · weak

Weak winds: the transition there rides mostly on thegas-solar hybrid, wind is not relevant there.

Solar + wind + storage hybrid

By day, the sun; by night and in the windy season, the turbine; storage smooths the rest. A mini-grid designed for the oases, mining sites and off-grid villages of the north — where pulling a line from the central grid makes no sense. All capabilities presented as GreenTech pole objectives; no assets in operation to date.

For our remote sites, autonomy is the rule: dedicated micro-grids (solar by day · associated gas + battery by night) power our sites, mini-refineries and depots — and every litre of generator diesel avoided removes the most expensive, most carbon-heavy energy of our operations.

A continental framework already under way: Chad hosts a national solar plant (~32 MW, in service — sector data) and takes part in Desert to Power (African Development Bank) — 10 GW of solar across the Sahel for 250 million people by 2030. GreenTech aligns with this momentum for its own energy needs and its ESG commitments.

Geological setting

Chad’s subsoil belongs to the Central African intracontinental rift system, formed in the Lower Cretaceous along the Central African shear zone (Central African Shear Zone, CASZ). Extension generated a series of half-grabens — Doba, Bongor, Doséo, Salamat and Termit — filled with lacustrine and fluvial sediments rich in source rocks.

The reservoirs are mostly Cretaceous sandstones (syn- and post-rift) at 1,000 to 2,500 m depth; locally, fractured granitic basement forms a secondary reservoir (Bongor basin). Crudes range from heavy and discounted (21–24° API at Doba) to lighter (~31° API at Bongor).

HSE-Q · Operational biodiversity

Protecting wildlife and flora, site by site.

In Chad, oil activity sits alongside fragile environments — Sahelian savannahs, the Logone and Chari wetlands, the northern deserts. EnerTchad builds the protection of living things into the very design of its sites, following the hierarchy avoid → reduce → restore.

01 · Avoid

Siting that steers around living things

Impact study (ESIA) and fauna-flora inventory before any work; mapping of sensitive habitats; siting outside protected areas (Zakouma, OROA, Ennedi) and outside migration corridors.

02 · Reduce

The smallest possible footprint

Modular mini-refineries and mobile assets = less land artificialised. Control of flaring and light pollution (migratory birds), tracks controlled against poaching, strict waste and hydrocarbon management, zero abstraction from critical aquifers.

03 · Restore

Give the site back to the environment

Revegetation with local species (Acacia gum trees, Sahelian flora), soil restoration and long-term biodiversity monitoring — with a “no net loss” target.

HSE-Q · Process safety

Prevent the accident, don’t record it.

Occupational safety protects the person; process safety prevents the major event — leak, fire, explosion. EnerTchad designs it upstream, through barriers that are independent, verified and maintained over time.

Control

Nothing changes without sign-off

Management of change (MOC) and work permits: no process modification or hot work without a risk analysis and a traced authorisation.
