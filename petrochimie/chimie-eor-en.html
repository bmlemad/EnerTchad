Oilfield chemistry · in development

Beyond products, the chemistry that makes crude flow.

Petrochemistry transforms the molecule on the way out; oilfield chemistry acts upstream, in the well and the pipe. The Petrochemicals pole aims to give Chad both faces of the same value chain — from EOR inputs to production fluids.

ENHANCED RECOVERY

EOR chemistry · ASP

The heart of “oilfield chemistry”: the alkali-surfactant-polymer (ASP) agents that mobilise residual crude. Alkali on local natron (Na₂CO₃), biopolymers and surfactants — the inputs that make EOR work best, and that we want to produce in Chad (Exploration & Production link).

Feedstocks from Chadian soil · sovereign sourcing

From Chadian soil to the heart of the reservoir.

Enhanced recovery (ASP, MEOR) consumes alkalis, surfactants and polymers — imported today. Chad holds most of these resources in its subsoil and its crops. Our ambition: transform them on the spot into branded feedstocks, packed in multi-format bags, for a double outlet — the farmer’s field and the oil well.

Reservoir side · Exploration & Production pole

The detailed formulation of these feedstocks — natron as the alkali, gum arabic and cellulose as polymers, neem & shea as biosurfactants, spirulina for MEOR — is decoded where it acts: on the Exploration & Production pole’s Enhanced recovery page.

Sourcing geography · source zones

Where Chad draws its feedstocks.

Four broad regions concentrate the resources: the Lake and Kanem (minerals & algae), the Sahelian belt (gum arabic), the Sudanian South (crops) and the oil basins (clays, sulphur).

Economic impact of the zones · targets

Sourcing in Chad keeps four regions alive.

Every feedstock bought at home feeds a local chain: gum pickers in the Sahel, salt workers in Kanem, cotton growers in the South, spirulina cooperatives at the Lake. The Petrochemicals pole does not just produce — it structures a new, stable, well-paying demand for resources that have been under-valued until now.
