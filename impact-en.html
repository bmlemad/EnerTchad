Chad’s realities · our ESG answer

An ESG anchored in reality, not on paper.

EnerTchad does not copy an imported grid: every commitment answers a concrete reality of the country — water, the Sahelian climate, youth employment, transparency of the rent. (target answers · company in formation)

Environment
A fragile, Sahelian environment
  • Reality — Lake Chad has lost ~90 % of its surface since 1960.→ Strict water management: produced water reinjected or reused, minimal net abstraction targeted.
  • Reality — A Sahel exposed to desertification and climate stress.→ End of routine flaring targeted (2030), site solar, carbon intensity lowered.
  • Reality — Arid soils, biodiversity under pressure.→ Site and corridor integrity, biodiversity monitoring, waste recovery (circular economy).
Social
A young country with great needs
  • Reality — Electricity access among the lowest in the world, above all in rural areas.→ Energy as the first domino: feeding industry, health, education and mobility.
  • Reality — A large young population, scarce skilled jobs.→ 80 % local content targeted, the Tchaditude academy — train before extracting.
  • Reality — Communities neighbouring the oil zone (Doba).→ Local purchasing and jobs first, shared infrastructure, continuous dialogue.
Governance
Transparency as the bedrock
  • Reality — A sensitive memory of how the oil rent was managed.→ Chadian capital, profits reinvested in-country — a rent that circulates, not one that leaks.
  • Reality — Accountability demands from lenders and the State.→ EITI targeted, OHADA · IFRS accounts, annual external audits.
  • Reality — The extractive sector’s corruption risk.→ ISO 37001 targeted (anti-bribery), code of ethics, whistleblower channel.

Water · Water-to-Value™

Treating and valorising produced water (water cut >85 %): reinjection, zero discharge targeted, piezometric monitoring of aquifers around every future site.

Forest & biodiversity

Deposit-based LPG against wood energy: every bottle exchanged spares trees, gum trees and pasture — biodiversity defended by the business model, not by offsets.

Site restoration

Decommissioning and restoration provisioned from each asset’s development plan — end of life funded from day one.

Local content

Capturing the value in Chad, not just the barrel.

Local content is not a regulatory constraint: it is our model. Maximising the Chadian share of jobs, purchases and skills at every link of the chain, so the oil industry feeds the national economy for good.

Chadian employment

Our commitment: target 80 % Chadian staff, with priority given to regional recruitment around each operating site. The national industry counts close to 1,240 direct jobs today.

Suppliers & SMEs

Subcontracting and purchasing steered towards Chadian companies: logistics, maintenance, catering, construction, services. See our supplier requirements or offer your services.

Skills transfer

Technical training, upskilling and know-how transfer to build national oil expertise.

Local inputs

Natron, gum arabic, cotton cellulose: our EOR inputs sourced in Chad create a downstream agricultural and mining value chain.

Local-content trajectory in our purchasing target 25 % by 2030

In line with the “Tchad Connexion 2030” plan, we progressively raise the share of Chadian goods and services in our spending — on the model of local-content policies that took some countries from under 5 % to over 50 % in two decades.

Rural world · Farming & herding

Energy, the bottleneck of agro-pastoral modernisation.

Most Chadians live from the land and the herd — one of Africa’s largest livestock populations. Without reliable energy there is no motor pump, no mill, no cold chain, no fairly priced transport. Our model lifts that bottleneck, piece by piece.

The first domino · Energy & purchasing power

From expensive energy to the fair price.

Energy precarity is not just a comfort problem: it makes the whole industrial chain more expensive. A factory running on costly diesel passes that premium into its prices. Reversing the equation means giving purchasing power back.

The vicious circle today
01Energy precarityGrid absent or unstable: industry cannot count on the power supply.
→
02Costly backup energyDiesel generators: costly, imported fuel at volatile prices.
→
03Inflated production costEvery manufactured good carries a heavy energy bill.
→
04Costlier finished goodsThe premium mechanically lands on the consumer.
The EnerTchad reversal ↓
Abundant, cheap energy → fair priceRefining locally, distributing at the ARSAT price everywhere and firming up the power supply (gas-solar hybrid) squeezes the production cost — and so the price of finished goods. Energy becomes an engine of competitiveness, not a dead weight.
Distribution at the fair price →Reliable site power →

The vision of a company in formation — the chain describes an economic mechanism, not an achieved result.

Mechanisation becomes plannable

Mobile Station™ in the farming season — ploughing, harvests, transhumance — at the same ARSAT price: the farmer in Mongo pays like the city dweller in N’Djamena.

Water for fields and herds

Reliable fuel for irrigation pumps and pastoral boreholes: off-season farming, water points along the transhumance routes.

Cooking that spares the trees

Deposit-based LPG against wood energy: pasture, gum trees and land preserved — herding and farming gain directly.

Yields under control

Urea/NPK fertilisers from gas (2030 horizon), asphalt roads to the markets, rural jobs — DODO, mobile operators, Tchaditude.

Modernising herding and farming without nearby energy is impossible — our mobile network is the agricultural infrastructure Chad was waiting for — and where energy arrives, the school and the health centre follow.

Circular economy · The Chadian double loop

Nothing is lost — neither matter nor capital.

EnerTchad is a Chadian company, with Chadian capital. Its circular-economy logic pursues two inseparable loops: putting every co-product to use from the oil chain in Chad, and having the capital reside and reinvest in-country — instead of exporting raw matter and repatriating the rent. (target model · company in formation)

The matter loop
Every co-product aims for a local outlet.
  • Associated gas → fertilisers & methanol (Petrochemicals), instead of being flared.
  • Desulphurisation sulphur → agricultural and industrial inputs.
  • Produced water → reinjection and reuse.
  • Heavy residues → bitumen for Chadian roads.
  • Used oils & waste → recovery channels.
The capital loop
Where the rent leaks elsewhere, here it circulates.
  • A company with Chadian capital: funds domiciled and reinvested in-country.
  • Suppliers, subcontractors and local jobs first (local content targeted, dated).
  • Financing and deposits in the national banking system.
  • Taxes and royalties to the State budget.
  • Profits reinvested in Chad, not repatriated abroad.
